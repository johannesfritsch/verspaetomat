# 10 — The feeling of the app

## The one image

The German station clock. Its red second hand runs to twelve, stops, waits a moment, and only then does the minute hand move. Every station in the country has a clock that visibly waits. That is Verspätomat: calm, precise, on your side, and unbothered by the fact that things take longer than planned.

The app does not fight the railway and it does not sell the customer a fantasy of punctuality. It takes the one thing the customer has in abundance, waiting time, and gives it weight.

## Tone of voice

- **German, "du", short.** Copy reads like the good conductor's announcement, not like a complaint form. "RE 7 kommt 14 Minuten später. Du auch. 14 Punkte."
- **Dry, never snide.** Rail vernacular is the humour: "Stellwerksstörung", "Personen im Gleis", "Gegenzug abwarten". The joke is that these phrases exist, not that anyone is incompetent.
- **Never angry.** The app does not say "schon wieder" and never invites the customer to rant. Anger is what every other delay experience already offers.
- **Honest about money.** "1,50 € Anspruch, ausgezahlt ab 4 €" is said plainly, every time. Nothing is called "gespendet" until the railway's reply confirms it. The community counter never shrinks because we promised too early.
- **Ceremony where it counts.** Signing your name on a claim is a small ritual, with a full-screen form and a real signature stroke. Everything else is two taps.

## Look

- **One ground.** Paper white with a slight cool tint, near-black ink, and one red: the second hand. No dark mode, no night variant. The ride screen is the same paper as everything else; what changes is the size of the number.
- **Numbers are the typography.** Minutes and euros are set large in a condensed face with tabular digits, the way a board sets them. Everything else is quiet.
- **One motion.** The delay counts up once, at arrival. The station clock's second hand sweeps and waits at twelve on the idle home screen. Nothing else moves.
- **No mascots, no confetti.** The reward for a 40-minute delay is a large, calm "40" and a line of text that makes you exhale.
- **Colour means state.** Green: on time. A red "+" before the minutes: late. Red text: "Ausfall", a rejected claim, an expiring deadline. Red is the accent and the alarm at once, so it is used sparingly: the plus sign, the second hand, the one thick rule, the progress dots. Never as a fill behind text.

## Sound and touch

- Check-in: the two-tone station chime, soft, optional.
- Delay grows: a single soft haptic. No sound.
- Arrival: a warmer three-tone. This is the one moment the app is allowed to feel like a reward.
- Claim sent: silence and a full-screen "Abgeschickt." with the date. Some things are better quiet.

## The emotional arc of one journey

1. **Nudge.** You have been standing on the platform for three minutes. The phone says: "Hauptbahnhof? Check dich ein." Helpful, not needy. Dismissable forever.
2. **Check-in.** Two taps: the train, the stop where you get off. The board chime. You put the phone away.
3. **Ride.** If you look, a lock-screen line shows the train, the next stop, the delay. It never asks for anything.
4. **Arrival.** The reveal. Minutes waited, points earned, and, if it was a long one, a line that says a claim now exists and who it will go to.
5. **Claim.** Weeks later, three delays have piled up. The app says the bundle is ready. You check the details, sign, and press send. A copy lands in your own inbox. A minute of your life.
6. **Confirmation.** The railway answers. The reply arrives in your inbox and in the app at the same moment. The number on the "Wir" screen ticks up, and it was you.

## Principles for every screen

- **One thumb, moving train.** Big targets, bottom sheets, nothing that needs two hands or a steady floor.
- **Works offline.** Check-in, ride view and ledger show the last known state and say how old it is.
- **Never feels like tracking.** Location is used at the station and only at the station. The ride screen says so, once, in small print.
- **Never blocks.** No permission is required to use the core loop. Manual check-in is a first-class path, not a fallback.
- **Every number has a source.** Tap any delay, any euro, any total, and a small sheet says where it came from and how fresh it is.
- **The customer presses send.** The app prepares and carries the mail; the customer signs, sends, and holds a copy of everything, in and out. This is a principle, not only a legal rule: it keeps the gift theirs, and it keeps us a messenger.
