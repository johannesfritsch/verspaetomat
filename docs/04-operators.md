# 04 — Who the claim goes to

## The rule

**The claim goes to whoever ran the late train, never to whoever sold the ticket.** A D-Ticket bought from a Verkehrsverbund or from Transdev used on a late DB Regio train is a claim against DB. A DB-sold D-Ticket on a late Transdev train is a claim against Transdev. DB's FAQ: submit collected cases to the Servicecenter Fahrgastrechte "oder der Fahrgastrechte-Abteilung des genutzten Eisenbahn-Verkehrsunternehmens".

## The joint Servicecenter scheme

The Servicecenter Fahrgastrechte (60647 Frankfurt am Main, operated by DB Dialog GmbH) is a joint claims desk of DB and about 40 non-federal railways that agreed on a common procedure ("gemeinsames Beschwerdeverfahren"). Same form, same address for all of them. Claims that land there for a non-participant are forwarded, but DB says contacting that operator directly is faster.

## Exceptions

Transdev is handled per subsidiary. Only "Transdev Verkehr GmbH" is on the list. The RheinRuhrBahn form points to the Servicecenter; other Transdev documents name Transdev Service GmbH, Passage 3-5, 17034 Neubrandenburg, or an online form at rhein-ruhr-bahn.de/fahrgastrechte. NordWestBahn, Bayerische Regiobahn, Mitteldeutsche Regiobahn and Transdev Hannover need individual verification.

Any operator not on the list (check current DB PDF before launch) needs its own entry: postal address, whether it publishes an e-mail for the EU form, whether it uses the joint form.

## What the app needs

- Every check-in records the operating company (live timetable feeds carry the agency per trip).
- Each operating company maps to a claims desk: "Servicecenter" or a named exception.
- Ledger bundles are grouped by desk.
- An operator directory maintained as content, not code: roughly 40 entries under one address plus a handful of exceptions.
